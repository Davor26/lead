## Confirmed against sources
- **Stirling, Laidre & Born (2021), *Arctic*.** They reviewed Inuit and historical accounts of polar bears throwing ice or rocks at walruses' heads. They concluded tool use is infrequent and mainly linked to walruses. A rebuttal letter was published in the same journal, which the script mentions ("Not every scientist agrees").
- **GoGo,** a young male polar bear at Tennoji Zoo in Osaka, threw sticks (and a tyre) at food hung out of reach. Some sources credit the GoGo paper to other authors, cited by Stirling et al., so the script only says "the scientists also pointed to".
- **Svalbard, July 2015.** A mother bear with a cub sniffed sleeping walruses, touched one with her paw, and retreated when it woke. Jon Aars (Norwegian Polar Institute) said she was likely testing for a weak animal. The footage is National Geographic's, so we illustrate it with your AI image and a diagram.
- **Wrangel Island, autumn 1990.** Ovsyanikov & Kochnev observed the haul-outs. Ovsyanikov (1995) reports that 11 attacks (44%) were on adults, and that bears preyed mostly on young animals.
- **Walrus mortality on Wrangel.** Kochnev (2002) found that most walrus deaths on the haul-outs came from stampedes, and that bears were the main cause of those stampedes.
- **Point Lay 2014:** about 35,000 walruses. **Icy Cape 2009:** more than 100 young walruses trampled (USGS). **Pacific walrus:** about 250,000 (2017 estimate of about 257,000). **IUCN:** Vulnerable.
- **Feeding.** Walruses can eat 3,000 to 6,000 clams in one feeding and use their tongue as a suction piston. Tusks are elongated canines, up to about 1 m in large males.

## Please verify (taken from your brief, not confirmed by me)
1. **"25 attempts → 2 kills → both calves"** (Ch. 7). This is consistent with Ovsyanikov 1995 (11 of 25 = 44% on adults), but I couldn't confirm "2 kills".
2. **"~43% of carcasses = calves"** (Ch. 7). I found no source for this exact figure.
3. **"Wrangel 1991 — 104 trampled"** (Ch. 13). A 1994 paper mentions about 100 carcasses packed together, but I couldn't confirm the year or the number 104.

## Wording choices to check
- Male polar bear "around six hundred kilograms", and walrus "more than twice as much". Large males reach about 1,200–1,500 kg, so this is approximate.
- Walrus songs: individual males' songs and mother–calf voice recognition are both reported in the literature, but kept general here.
- "Since 2007, the summer ice has retreated beyond the shelf in most years." This is the widely reported pattern, but please confirm the wording.
